Hi! My name is Ailing Jin. I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Geography, Sustainability, Community, and Urban Studies at the University of Connecticut, expecting to graduate in December 2026.

My research studies where and how people are exposed to air pollution in their daily lives. I combine GIS, mobile and real-time sensing, GPS tracking, and indoor positioning (Bluetooth Low Energy beacons) to measure personal exposure to PM2.5 indoors and outdoors, and to estimate the dose people actually inhale as they move and stay active.

My dissertation is titled Fine-Scale Assessment of Personal Exposure to Indoor and Outdoor Air Pollution Using Geospatial and Real-Time Sensing Technologies.

I hold an MS in Health Promotion Sciences from UConn, an MA in Geography from Binghamton University (SUNY), and a BS in Ecology from Minzu University of China.

    Papers in Progress

      Research

      2025 – present

      Fine-Scale Spatiotemporal Modeling of Indoor Air Pollution and Personal Exposure

      Department of GSCU, University of Connecticut

      • Built indoor air sensor networks in participants' homes, paired with Bluetooth Low Energy beacon systems, to monitor indoor mobility and PM2.5 in real time.
      • Model spatiotemporal variation of indoor air pollution and create indoor air quality maps.
      • Generate personalized exposure reports with GIS and share them with participants.
      2023 – 2025

      Mobility- and Inhaled Dose-Based PM2.5 Exposure and Health Risk Assessment

      Department of GSCU, University of Connecticut

      • Integrated real-time indoor and outdoor PM2.5 monitoring with GPS tracking, physical activity intensity, and physiological characteristics.
      • Compared exposure metrics across population groups and developed 3D geovisualizations of exposure dynamics.
      2021 – 2023

      Community Food Environment Assessment Using Mobile Phone Location Data

      Department of Allied Health Sciences, University of Connecticut

      • Related community socioeconomic factors to restaurant visits with multivariate regression, revealing selective daily mobility bias in community food environments.
      2019 – 2021

      Storefront Transparency Evaluation Using Google Street View and Deep Learning

      Department of Geography, Binghamton University (SUNY)

      • Extracted window and wall information from street-level images to evaluate building transparency along Main Street.
